Library Etiquette

  • Keep it tidy: Please help keep the library area clean and organized.
  • Check for pests: Before donating, please take a moment to inspect your plants to ensure they are healthy and free of pests.
  • Hardy plants only: Delicate plants may not survive in the box for long, so aim for sharing hardy plants through the box itself. You can arrange pickups or drop-offs in WhatsApp for plants that might not do well sitting for a few days.
  • Label everything: Please label any cuttings or seeds you share back to the library if you know the plant or variety name!
  • Return pots and tags: If you are finished with your starter pots or plant tags, please feel free to drop them back off in the box so they can be reused for the next batch.

What to Avoid

  • Toxic plants: Please avoid donating plants that are known to be toxic to pets, such as lilies or ivy.
  • Invasive species: Please refrain from donating plants that are considered invasive in our local ecosystem.

General Quick-Start Tips for Succulents

  • Soil: Plant in well-draining cactus or succulent soil mix.
  • Light: Most succulents thrive in bright, indirect light or gentle morning sun.
  • Water: Always let the soil dry out completely between waterings. When in doubt, wait a few extra days.

Featured Plants & Care Guides

Jade Plant (Crassula ovata)

  • Light: Bright light with some direct sun.
  • Water: Water deeply, then let the soil dry out entirely (roughly every 2–3 weeks depending on climate).
  • Tip: If thick leaves start looking slightly wrinkled, your jade is ready for a drink.

Dark Burgundy Aeonium (Aeonium arboreum 'Zwartkop')

  • Light: Bright indirect light to partial sun (protect from harsh, scorching afternoon heat).
  • Water: Water when the top inch of soil feels dry. They enjoy more moisture during active growing season (fall to spring – they go dormant in summer).
  • Tip: These feature dark, glossy rosettes, though they may fade to green in lower light.

Ghost Plant (Graptopetalum paraguayense)

  • Light: Plenty of bright light to bring out their color.
  • Water: Highly drought-tolerant, err on the side of underwatering.
  • Tip: Handle gently as leaves drop easily, but they are great for propagation (see below).

Hardy Ice Plant (Delosperma cooperi)

  • Light: Full sun to partial shade for best blooms.
  • Water: Water moderately when established, allowing soil to dry out between waterings.
  • Tip: Great for spreading across a sunny garden bed or spilling over container edges. (Note: Avoid the invasive Carpobrotus edulis).

How to Propagate Succulents to Share Back

Got extra succulents and want to contribute to the library? Here are two simple ways to propagate them:

1. Stem Cuttings

  • Snip: Cut a healthy 2 to 4-inch piece from the top or side of a mature plant using clean, sharp shears.
  • Callus: Place the cutting in a dry, shaded spot for a few days until the cut end forms a dry, hardened callus (prevents rotting).
  • Plant & Root: Stick the callused end directly into dry succulent soil. Hold off on watering for a week or two until tiny roots form, then water sparingly.

2. Leaf Propagation

  • Pluck: Gently twist a healthy, plump leaf off the stem, making sure it breaks off cleanly right at the base.
  • Scab Over: Lay leaves flat on a dry paper towel or directly on top of dry soil in a tray out of direct sunlight.
  • Grow: Over a few weeks, tiny roots and baby plantlets will sprout from the base. Mist lightly only after roots appear.

Note: Not all succulents propagate well from leaves. Aeoniums in particular do not do well with leaf propagation.
